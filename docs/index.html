The Structural Frame

Fifteen techniques are useless without a skeleton to hang them on. This is the beat sequence underneath a premium article — nine positions, each with a job. Click any beat to see what belongs there and which effects serve it.

Why this comes first: A hook's authority ends the moment it opens the loop. Everything after that is structure. Most web writing fails not at the opener but at beat four, where the reader's initial curiosity has been spent and nothing has replaced it.

Effect 01

Scroll-Triggered Reveal

Elements stay invisible until they enter the viewport, then fade and rise into place. An IntersectionObserver watches each block and adds a class — no scroll listener, no jank.

Why it hooks: Movement at the edge of vision triggers an orienting response. The reader's scroll caused the reveal, creating a reward loop that pulls the thumb down again.

Effect 02

Sticky Scrollytelling

A city map pins to the viewport while text panels scroll past it. Each panel pans, zooms, or labels the same map — the scroll becomes a guided investigation.

Why it hooks: The reader keeps one visual model in memory while each step adds meaning: first location, then focus, then evidence.

Effect 04

“You Draw It” Canvas

Drag across the blank half of the chart to predict the trend. Release, then reveal the truth.

Why it hooks: Commitment bias plus the generation effect. Once you've drawn a guess you have stakes, and the reveal lands emotionally.

Effect 08

Parallax Depth

Six layers translate at different rates against the scroll. Distant layers barely move; near layers move most, which the brain reads as physical depth.

Why it hooks: Implied depth signals production value within the first second, justifying the reader's time investment before a word is read.

Effect 09

Color-Threaded Entities

A colour introduced in prose reappears in every chart, map and timeline that follows. The reader builds a private visual vocabulary and never needs a legend.

Why it hooks: Each recurrence produces a small recognition reward. The technique operates continuously rather than as discrete moments of spectacle, which is what makes a page feel authored.
Live — tap, hover, or focus any coloured name, bar, dot or band

Effect 10

Binary Quiz Accumulator

Twelve statements, one tap each. Lowering the cost of a single interaction is what lets you raise the number of interactions.

Why it hooks: Sunk-cost accumulation. By the fifth answer the reader has invested enough to want the result, and the progress bar makes that investment visible.

Effect 11

Turn-by-Turn Procedural Graphic

One continuous action, decomposed into five inspectable phases. The reader can watch it run, or stop and interrogate any single stage.

Why it hooks: Expertise is invisible at full speed. Slowing a motion down and labelling its parts converts a spectator into someone who can see what the athlete is doing — a genuine transfer of knowledge, not just spectacle.

Effect 13

Before / After Comparison

A draggable divider between two states of the same scene. The reader performs the comparison rather than being told the conclusion.

Why it hooks: Change is hard to perceive from two images side by side, but trivial when they occupy the same pixels. The reader's hand does the analysis.

Effect 14

Audio Waveform & Transcript Sync

A scrubable waveform bound to a transcript. Clicking any line jumps the playhead; the active line highlights as it plays.

Why it hooks: It makes audio skimmable. Readers who would never sit through eight minutes will happily jump to the sentence that interests them — and often stay.
